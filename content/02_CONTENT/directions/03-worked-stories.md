## 1. Coma — `sleeping can put you in a coma`

**Family:** 01-coma-myth  
**Path:** ALL · land on `how-much-sleep-do-you-actually-need`  
**Format:** 15s Reel. Same spine as Debunk.

### Hook (0:00)

On screen, no wink, no "myth:":

> sleeping can put you in a coma

Optional second turn (0:03):

> I slept 11 hours and I still felt gone

Do not smile. Do not put "not true" on this frame.

### Story

**Unsell (0:06)**  
That is not what this is. Sleeping does not put you in a coma.

**Mix-up (0:08)**  
People say "out cold" and "dead asleep" like those are medical. They are language. A coma is a different state. Sleep is a night you can still be woken from.

**Real info (0:09)**  
In sleep you still move through stages. An alarm, light, a hand on the shoulder — people wake. Heavy sleep is still sleep. The hours on the clock are not a spell that takes you somewhere you cannot leave.

**Tonight (0:12)**  
If the rumor started because you slept a long time and felt worse, do not collect more hours as a dare. Write three lines in the morning for a week: bedtime, wake time, one word for how the morning felt. The diary is ruder and more useful than the scare. Do not use the clock like a dose.

**Cannot promise**  
A diary will not explain every heavy morning. If someone cannot be woken, or long sleep is new and frightening, that is a clinician, not this video. This is not medical advice.

### Caption (draft)

Sleeping cannot put you in a coma. "Out cold" is a sentence, not a state. Sleep is an active night — stages still run, and you can be woken. A coma is something else. If a long night left you feeling gone, do not use the clock like a dose. Notice the week you actually had. Not medical advice. Save this if you needed the distinction, not the scare.

### Debunk slides (9)

1. sleeping can put you in a coma  
2. I slept 11 hours and still felt gone  
3. that is not what this is  
4. sleep is not a coma  
5. "out cold" is language. you can still be woken.  
6. sleep still has stages. the night is not an off switch.  
7. the hours are not a spell  
8. tonight: three lines in the morning, one week  
9. this cannot promise a diagnosis. not medical advice.

Slide 1 is the hook. Slides 3–4 are the unsell. If you print only slide 1, you have not made the post.

## 2. Eyes — `my eyes are red after sleep`

**Family:** 04-eyes-didnt-return  
**Path:** ALL · land on `bedroom-temperature-18c-is-a-starting-point` / `light-lamps-and-the-bedroom`  
**Implied rumor to unsell:** red eyes mean I did not come all the way back.

### Hook (0:00)

> my eyes are red after sleep

Second turn (0:03):

> my eyes are red because I did not come all the way back

### Story

**Unsell**  
That is not leftover unconsciousness. You woke. The eyes are not proof you stayed under.

**Mix-up**  
Morning face looks like a verdict. It is usually a room: dry air, salt, heat, the side you slept on, a late drink, the hours you actually got — not a failed return.

**Real info**  
Eyelids can look worse after a night you did take. Fluid sits. Air dries. A warm room puffs. That is boring and it is more useful than a scare.

**Tonight**  
Cool the room toward a starting point you can stand. One warm lamp, not the overhead. Water on the table, not a story about your soul.

**Cannot promise**  
This will not clear every morning face. If redness is new, painful, or only one eye and it worries you, that is not a sleep tip.

### Caption (draft)

Red eyes after sleep are not proof you failed to wake. Morning face is often the room — dry air, heat, salt, position — not leftover night. Cool the bedroom a little. Dim to one lamp. Notice the week, not one bathroom. Not medical advice. Save this for the next time the mirror starts a rumor.

## 3. Dare — `why should I sleep`

**Family:** 09-why-leave  
**Path:** FOCUS / ALL · land on `how-much-sleep-do-you-actually-need`  
**Implied rumor to unsell:** sleep is just leaving, so staying up is how you keep a life.

### Hook (0:00)

> why should I sleep

Second turn (0:03):

> why should I sleep if sleep is just leaving

### Story

**Unsell**  
The mood is real. The conclusion is not. Sleep is not a theft of your only hours, and staying up is not how you stay a person.

**Mix-up**  
The last quiet hour feels like the only room you own. So the night looks like a door closing. That is a want, not evidence that rest is optional.

**Real info**  
How much sleep you need is not a moral number. It is closer to a week you can notice than a dare you win at 1am. The day you actually have still has to be entered.

**Tonight**  
Close one loop before the dark: tomorrow's first task on one paper, laptop out of the room. You are not disappearing. You are ending a shift.

**Cannot promise**  
This will not make 11pm feel fair. It will not give you a private house in the morning.

### Caption (draft)

"Why should I sleep" is a real sentence. It is not proof that sleep is optional, or that the night is only leaving. The quiet hour is a want. The morning still arrives. Close one loop — one task on paper, laptop out — then take the night you actually have. Not medical advice. Save this if you needed permission to stop performing the day.
